There is a particular smell to a print shop in the late spring. The humidity shifts, the ink sits differently on the drum, and the paper absorbs the weight of the air. This month, we've been overseeing the production of a 300-page monograph, and the analog friction has been a welcome retreat from our screens.

In a purely digital studio practice, the finality of print is terrifying. Cmd+Z doesn't exist when the plates are burning. Every typographic decision becomes permanent. We spent hours agonizing over the tracking of a single caption on page 42, knowing that once the cylinders rolled, that decision would live on shelves for decades.

We often ask ourselves how we can bring this same level of deliberation back to the web. If deploying code carried the same financial and physical weight as running a Heidelberg press, how would our interfaces change? Would we build with more restraint?
